# Removing Shame and Aligning Security Incentives

# Thesis

There are a number of “somewhat socially acceptable” crimes today that are used by malicious actors to cause their victims to under-report.  Regardless of personal morals, there are efforts underway to decriminalize the purchase of personal use illicit substances as well as decriminalization of pay-for-sex.  These are two high-shame, non-violent crimes that provide cover for organized crime to operate with impunity.  Drug addiction is even covered by a number of disability / medical laws, yet the ability for these high-shame activities to be leveraged for blackmail is shockingly high.

This is effective for the hiding of criminals due to the “no true Scotsman” logical fallacy.  By ensuring that the victim of a larger crime has committed at least a small one, they are able to create underreporting.

This existing is guilty of the “moralistic fallacy”.  We believe that these crimes do not exist, and do not take into account that they do.  If we were able to create a system of self reporting for these interactions, with an explicit guarantee that the data would not be used in procession (fifth amendment right against self incrimination) but instead as a contact tracing surveillance network to overlay other forms of traditional surveillance, we could find the hidden parts of the network.  This naturally would not be blanket immunity, as state or other charges could come about, but effective trust that a data source is meant to find systemic crime rather than individuals must be maintained.

The use of such a system might be compulsory for cleared persons, and voluntary for other non-cleared high value targets.

# Implementation

Today we have “contact APIs” on our devices.  These allow applications to associate communications with a “person”.  By augmenting this with better per-application linkage, as well as an “upload” scheme whereby an app can transmit actors and metadata to the system, which can then use a delegated upload app, continuous and useful data can be transmitted (in a scheme similar to epidemiological contact tracing).  This reduces the need for malware to be used by our US government as a key SIGINT scheme.

This further might also provide the ability for an owner of a device to self export their data.  While this might seem antithetical to things like SnapChat, it doesn’t remove their existing “wipe after see” approach any more than the multitudes of jailbroken devices.
